# Case study writer

A recruiter skims for 30 seconds; a hiring manager reads for 5 minutes. Write for both.

## Structure

1. **At a glance**: problem, what I did, outcome. Three short paragraphs.
2. **The problem**: who struggles, with what, and the "How might we" that framed the work.
3. **Research**: methods and numbers (e.g. "5 interviews, 2 usability rounds, 12 participants"), then insights written as *finding → so what*.
4. **Key decisions**: each with what I chose **and what it cost**. Trade-offs are the most senior part of a case study.
5. **Design → code**: what I built, with one real snippet.
6. **Testing and iteration**: what I found, what I changed. Before/after where possible.
7. **Impact and learnings**: honest outcomes, plus how I'd measure next time.

## Voice

- First person, active, plain words. Short sentences.
- No buzzwords ("leverage", "seamless", "delightful", "robust").
- No emojis in body copy.

## Honesty rules

- Never invent metrics, quotes or participants. If a number isn't in the source material, ask for it or leave a placeholder.
- Label self-assessed or illustrative content as such.
- Name collaborators and what they did; don't take credit for team work.
